# Legal brief: website accessibility law for Wide Aisle

Task T-011 · M1 research lane · Checked 2026-10-08

**This is not legal advice.** It is research to prepare for the lawyer review the owner approved in D-14. Every figure below comes from a search summary we could not open (the sites were blocked from this container) unless it says "opened". Treat each one as a lead to verify, not a fact to quote.

## The answer first

- **US suits keep rising.** 3,117 website accessibility suits were filed in federal court in 2025, up 27% from 2024, and 36% of all ADA Title III federal filings [L1] (via search, not opened). Counts that add key state courts pass 5,000, and about 70% target ecommerce [L2] (via search, not opened). One vendor, citing UsableNet, says 2026 is on track for more than 6,000 [L4] (via search, not opened; unverified).
- **There is no federal web rule for private businesses.** Courts treat WCAG 2.1 AA as the working benchmark, and they disagree on whether a web-only store is covered at all [L5] (via search, not opened). Federal ADA claims give an injunction and attorney's fees, not damages. State laws such as California's Unruh Act add $4,000 per violation [L7] (via search, not opened).
- **Overlay widgets do not stop suits.** Suits named companies using accessibility widgets more than 1,400 times in 2025 [L2] (via search, not opened). In April 2025 the FTC approved a final order requiring accessiBe to pay $1 million and barring it from claiming its automated tool makes sites WCAG-conformant without evidence [L9] (via search, not opened).
- **The EU Accessibility Act applies to online stores selling to EU consumers since 28 June 2025.** Sources say it reaches sellers outside the EU (lawyer question 15). Microenterprises are exempt from the service rules. Covered stores must publish information on how the service meets the requirements. Fines are set country by country, up to €100,000 in Germany and up to €1,000,000 in Spain for the most serious cases [L12, L13] (via search, not opened). A French court ordered Carrefour in June 2026 to make its site and app accessible within six months [L15] (via search, not opened).
- **The US Title II rule covers state and local governments, not stores.** The April 2026 interim final rule moved its WCAG 2.1 AA deadlines to 26 April 2027 (50,000 people or more) and 26 April 2028 (smaller bodies and special districts) [L17] (via search, not opened). The National Federation of the Blind sued DOJ on 21 May 2026 to reverse the extension, so both dates are under challenge [L18] (via search, not opened). It matters for the M8 public-body option, not for v1.
- **For Wide Aisle:** demand is real, overclaiming is the main legal risk we control, and an EU accessibility information page is a product feature for EU merchants. The questions for a lawyer are at the end.

### How the law works, in plain terms

1. **The statute.** Title III of the ADA bars discrimination by businesses open to the public. It names no web standard. The Justice Department dropped its plan to write a web rule for private businesses in 2017 [L5] (via search, not opened).
2. **Guidance.** DOJ published web accessibility guidance in March 2022. In March 2025 DOJ withdrew 11 Title III guidance documents; the summaries we saw list none about the web, but we could not confirm the 2022 web guidance is still posted [L5, L10] (via search, not opened; unverified). Guidance does not have the force of a regulation.
3. **The standard courts use.** In practice, plaintiffs and courts measure sites against WCAG 2.1 AA [L5] (via search, not opened).
4. **Whether websites are covered.** Federal appeals courts are split. Some require a link to a physical place open to the public; others do not. The Ninth Circuit held in Robles v. Domino's (2019) that the ADA applied to Domino's website and app. A California appeals court held the ADA does not reach a business that exists only online [L5, L7] (via search, not opened).
5. **What a plaintiff can win.** Federal ADA claims give an order to fix the site plus attorney's fees, not money damages. California's Unruh Act adds a $4,000 minimum per violation; courts have mostly rejected counting each visit as a new violation. In New York, high-volume plaintiff firms moved to state court under state and city human rights laws [L7] (via search, not opened).
6. **Repeat suits.** Settling with one plaintiff does not stop the next. UsableNet counted 1,427 suits in 2025 against companies already sued [L2] (via search, not opened).

### Demand letters

- No public source counts them. Seyfarth notes that federal filing totals miss demand letters and many state suits [L2] (via search, not opened).
- Vendor sources say demands run from $5,000 to $50,000 or more, with 30 to 60 days to respond. They put small-business settlements at about $5,000 to $20,000, often less at the letter stage, and say defence fees can exceed the settlement [L11] (via search, not opened). These figures come from companies that sell remediation. Treat them as unverified.
- The roadmap asks what documentation helps most after a demand letter. Defence firms and vendors commonly point to dated scan history, a remediation log, an accessibility statement with a contact route, and evidence of manual testing. We found no neutral source that measures what works. This is question 5 for the lawyer.

## Overlays and the FTC order against accessiBe

**What overlays are.** A JavaScript widget added to a site that changes the page at run time or offers a toolbar. The underlying theme code stays as it was.

**What practitioners and users say.** The Overlay Fact Sheet, a statement by hundreds of accessibility practitioners, says overlays do not fix underlying problems. In a WebAIM survey, 67% of respondents rated overlays not effective, rising to 72% of respondents with disabilities [L19] (via search, not opened).

**Suits against widget users.** UsableNet's 2025 year-end figures, as reported, show suits named companies using accessibility widgets more than 1,400 times [L2] (via search, not opened).

**The FTC order.**

| Item | What we found |
|---|---|
| Parties | Federal Trade Commission and accessiBe (accessWidget plug-in) |
| Timeline | Complaint and proposed order announced 3 January 2025; final order approved April 2025, announced 22 April 2025; docket C-4817 [L9] |

| Money | $1 million. Most coverage calls it a fine; the order describes it as monetary relief, not a civil penalty [L9] |
| What the FTC alleged | Claims that the tool made any website WCAG-conformant were false, misleading or unsubstantiated; paid reviews were presented as independent [L9] |
| What the order bars | Claiming that an automated product can make any website WCAG-conformant, or keep it conformant over time, without evidence; misrepresenting that reviewers are independent [L9] |
| What the order requires | Summaries say accessiBe must disclose, before a customer pays, that the tool does not fix barriers on other domains or subdomains unless those also use it, and must disclose any material connection to endorsers near its product claims. | Admission | None. accessiBe neither admitted nor denied the allegations [L9] |

All rows: via search, not opened. The FTC's own documents (ftc.gov, docket C-4817) were blocked.

**Three lessons for us.**

1. The FTC looks at whether a claim is backed by evidence. Every number we publish needs a dated scan behind it. D-06 already bans "compliant" and its relatives.
2. Pilots get a founding discount. Their testimonials may need a disclosure of that connection. This is question 3 for the lawyer.
3. The roadmap and D-06 now say the FTC "ordered" accessiBe "to pay $1 million", which is closer to the order's wording than "fined".

## EU: the European Accessibility Act

### What it is and who it covers

| Item | What we found |
|---|---|
| Law | Directive (EU) 2019/882, put into national law by each member state (for example Germany's BFSG) [L12] |
| In force for services | 28 June 2025 [L12] |
| Covers | Ecommerce services to consumers, among other products and services [L12] |
| Non-EU sellers | Sources say it reaches sellers outside the EU when they sell to EU consumers, because the test is where the customer is (lawyer question 15) [L12] |
| Microenterprise exemption | Fewer than 10 staff and annual turnover or balance sheet of €2 million or less; exempt from the service requirements [L12] |
| De facto benchmark today | EN 301 549 V3.2.1, based on WCAG 2.1 AA. It is harmonised under the Web Accessibility Directive, which covers public bodies. No version of EN 301 549 is cited in the Official Journal under the EAA yet, so meeting V3.2.1 gives no presumption of conformity under the EAA. Guides still point to it as the benchmark until a version is cited under the EAA [L14, L20] |
| Next standard | EN 301 549 V4.1.1, based on WCAG 2.2 AA; adopted 24 August 2026, published 2 September 2026; Official Journal citation expected late November or mid-December 2026 [L14]. Check whether that citation is made under the EAA |

All rows: via search, not opened.

### The information duty ("accessibility statement")

- **Article 13** says service providers must prepare information under Annex V, explain how the service meets the accessibility requirements, make it public in written and oral format in a way people with disabilities can use, and keep it for as long as the service runs [L12] (via search of a UK copy of the EU text, not opened).
- **Annex V** says the information goes in the general terms and conditions or an equivalent document. It lists (a) a general description of the service in accessible formats and (b) descriptions and explanations needed to understand how the service works. The French text adds (c) a description of how the service meets the Annex I requirements; we could not confirm the English wording of (c) [L12] (via search, not opened; partly unverified).
- **Vendors call this an "accessibility statement".** The EAA does not set a template, as far as we found (unverified). The public-sector Web Accessibility Directive does: Implementing Decision (EU) 2018/1523 sets a model statement with five mandatory parts: compliance status (full, partial or not), non-accessible content and why, how and when the statement was prepared, a feedback route with a named contact, and the enforcement procedure [L16] (via search, not opened). That model is a sensible base for our statement generator, adapted to the Annex V items.

### Enforcement so far

- **France, private action.** In July 2025 two disability associations (apiDV and Droit Pluriel) sent formal notices to Auchan, Carrefour, E.Leclerc and Picard. In November 2025 they started fast-track court cases [L15] (via search, not opened).
- **Auchan, 5 May 2026.** The Lille court dismissed the case because Auchan's French ecommerce turnover was under the €250 million threshold in the 2005 law, while noting a low level of accessibility. The associations appealed to the Douai Court of Appeal [L15] (via search, not opened).
- **Carrefour, 4 June 2026.** The Caen court ordered carrefour.fr and its app to be made accessible within six months, under daily penalty. Carrefour argued that 71% of its site met the requirements; the court said a partly accessible shop was not enough [L15] (via search, not opened). These are interim orders and may change on the merits.
- **France, regulator.** On 4 September 2026 the DGCCRF reported that more than half of the ecommerce businesses it inspected this year showed shortcomings. Final results are expected in early 2027 [L15] (via search, not opened).

## Questions for a lawyer

For the D-14 review. Grouped so the lawyer can price them.

**Our claims and marketing**

1. Can we say "Wide Aisle fixed 212 of 240 detected issues on your key pages" with a dated scan, without implying the store conforms to WCAG? Which words in our draft claims policy (docs/policy/claims-policy.md) still carry risk?
2. Summaries of the accessiBe order say it must disclose, before a customer pays, that its tool does not fix other domains or subdomains. Our fixes do not reach Shopify checkout or app widgets. Should our listing and pricing page state those limits before a merchant pays, and where exactly?
3. Pilots pay a founding price of about 50% off. What disclosure do FTC endorsement rules require when we publish their testimonials or case studies?
4. Is "not a widget" or "real fixes in your theme code" a comparative claim about overlay vendors that needs extra substantiation?

**Our liability to merchants**

5. Which documents actually help a merchant answer a demand letter or suit in New York, California and Florida: scan history, remediation log, accessibility statement, known-limitations list? Could any of them be used against the merchant as an admission of known barriers?
6. If a merchant using Wide Aisle is sued, what claims could they bring against us (breach of warranty, negligence, misrepresentation)? What should our terms say on warranty disclaimers, limitation of liability and indemnity?
7. If a fix breaks a store's layout or sales, what do our terms need to cover, given the preview and one-click revert?
8. Does operating as an individual Shopify Partner for the pilots (D-15) expose personal assets to these claims? Is that a reason to form a company before G3?

**US law**

9. Is DOJ's March 2022 web guidance still in force after the March 2025 withdrawals, and does it change anything for us?
10. Should our reports reference WCAG 2.1 AA (what US courts use) or WCAG 2.2 AA (current W3C version), or both?
11. Is there legal risk in citing lawsuit statistics in outreach or ads to merchants, given D-16 already excludes sued stores?

**EU law**

12. For a covered EU merchant, is an accessibility information page linked from the footer and from the terms enough to meet Article 13 and Annex V? What does "oral format" require for an online store?
13. What must the information contain in Germany and France specifically, and must it be in the national language?
14. How should our statement generator treat microenterprises: should they publish voluntarily, and does that create obligations?
15. Does the EAA apply to a US or UK Shopify store that ships to EU consumers, and does it need an EU representative?
16. For French merchants below €250 million turnover, which threshold applies while the Auchan appeal is pending?
17. Does Wide Aisle itself (a business-to-business app, not a consumer service) have any EAA or ADA duties for its own admin app and marketing site?

**Operations**

18. Our M2 census reads public storefront pages of about 2,000 stores. Are there terms-of-service, computer-misuse or EU data protection issues, given we store theme names and scan results only?
